\subsection{Semantic and arithmetic facts used by the proof}
\label{subsec:main-proof-facts}
The structural induction uses the standard LTL expansions of always,
eventually, and weak Until. For every LTL formula $A$ and position $i$,
\begin{align}
\rho,i\satL \Always A &\Longleftrightarrow
  \forall j\ge i,\;\rho,j\satL A,\nonumber\\
\rho,i\satL \Eventually A &\Longleftrightarrow
  \exists j\ge i,\;\rho,j\satL A,\nonumber\\
\rho,i\satL \Always\Eventually A &\Longleftrightarrow
  \forall n\ge i\;\exists j\ge n,\;\rho,j\satL A,\label{eq:main-sem-GF}\\
\rho,i\satL A\WeakUntil B &\Longleftrightarrow
  \Bigl(\exists j\ge i.\;\rho,j\satL B\\[-1mm]
  &\qquad\land\forall k\in[i,j).\;\rho,k\satL A\Bigr)\nonumber\\[-1mm]
  &\qquad\lor\forall j\ge i.\;\rho,j\satL A.\nonumber
\end{align}
The Coq lemmas are \texttt{LG\_semantics}, \texttt{LF\_semantics},
\texttt{LGF\_semantics}, and \texttt{LW\_semantics}. Time divergence also
gives a future $B$ position at any required physical distance when
$\Always\Eventually B$ holds (\texttt{GF\_far}); this supplies the witness
in the fairness branch of lower-bounded Until.

\paragraph{Clock arithmetic.}
Clock consistency turns a guard on a clock into a bound on elapsed time.
\begin{lemma}[Accumulation under preservation]
\label{lem:clock-accumulation}
If $x$ is not reset in $[i,j)$, then
\[
\CVal_\rho(j,x)=\CVal_\rho(i,x)+\elapsed_w(i,j).
\]
\end{lemma}
Since clock values are nonnegative, this implies
\begin{equation}
\elapsed_w(i,j)\le\CVal_\rho(j,x),
\label{eq:main-preservation-elapsed}
\end{equation}
so an upper guard $x\le d$ yields
\begin{equation}
\elapsed_w(i,j)\le d.
\label{eq:upper-clock}
\end{equation}
The strict guard $x<d$ yields the corresponding strict inequality.

\begin{lemma}[Clock value after a reset]
\label{lem:clock-after-reset}
If $x$ is reset at $i$ and $j\ge i+1$, then
\[
\CVal_\rho(j,x)\le\elapsed_w(i,j),
\]
with equality if there is no later reset before $j$.
\end{lemma}
A later reset only makes the clock younger. Consequently, a lower guard
implies
\begin{equation}
x\ge d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d\le\CVal_\rho(j,x)\le\elapsed_w(i,j),
\label{eq:lower-clock}
\end{equation}
and $x>d$ gives a strict lower bound. Coq proves these facts as
\texttt{preserve\_accumulate},
\texttt{after\_reset\_value\_le\_elapsed}, and
\texttt{reset\_preserve\_value\_eq\_elapsed}.

\subsection{Strict-bound cases}
\label{subsec:main-strict-bounds}
The eight hatted constructors are the primitive proof families; ordinary
timed operators have already been reduced by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:derive-ule}--\eqref{eq:derive-rge}. Strict comparisons remain
strict through each translation. Preservation gives
$\elapsed_w(i,j)\le\CVal_\rho(j,x)$, so $x<d$ entails an elapsed time
strictly below $d$. After a reset,
$\CVal_\rho(j,x)\le\elapsed_w(i,j)$, so $x>d$ entails an elapsed time
strictly above $d$.

The fairness branch of $\hUntil_{>d}$ needs one additional step. Time
divergence gives a future $q$ at distance at least $d$ from position $i+1$;
strict timestamp increase gives $t_{i+1}>t_i$. The same witness is therefore
at distance strictly greater than $d$ from $i$. This is the argument of the
Coq lemma \texttt{sound\_UhatGt}.
