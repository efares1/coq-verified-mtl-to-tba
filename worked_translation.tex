\subsection{Worked example: translating a bounded response}
\label{subsec:worked-translation}
Consider
\[
  f=(\Eventually_{\le5}p)\land(\Always_{<2}\neg p).
\]
The property requires $p$ to be false at the temporal origin and at every
later event less than two time units from it, and requires a $p$ event by age
five. Figure~\ref{fig:translation-reference-tba} shows a hand-simplified,
equivalent one-clock event-guarded TBA. It is not the direct output of the
syntax-directed translation below, which assigns separate clocks to the two
distinct timed subformulas. The figure's first transition consumes the event
at the origin, requires $\neg p$, and resets $x$. Under the event-indexed
reset convention of Section~\ref{sec:tba}, the clock value at the next event
is therefore the elapsed time since the origin. The construction below
follows the formula through the clocked-LTL intermediate language and the
timed reinterpretation.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{0.88\columnwidth}{!}{\input{figures/fig_tba_example}}
\caption{A hand-simplified equivalent event-guarded TBA for $f$. The initial transition
requires $\neg p$ and resets $x$. In the waiting location, every outgoing
transition checks $x\le5$; the transition to the accepting location also
requires $2\le x$. Thus $p$ can first hold only at an event whose age from
the origin is in $[2,5]$.}
\label{fig:translation-reference-tba}
\end{figure}

\begin{enumerate}[label=Step~\arabic*:,leftmargin=*]
\item The formula is in negation normal form. Expanding the derived operators
and applying the timed-operator identities gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
f
 &= (\top\Until_{\le5}p)\land(\bot\Release_{<2}\neg p)\\
 &= \bigl(p\lor(\top\land\top\hUntil_{\le5}p)\bigr)\\
 &\qquad\land\bigl(\neg p\land(\bot\lor\bot\hRelease_{<2}\neg p)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The current-position alternatives in the last line are the derived-operator
cases of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:derive-ule} and~\eqref{eq:derive-rle}.

\item The two distinct primitive timed subformulas receive clocks $x$ and
$y$, respectively. Their clocked-LTL translation is
\begin{align*}
\Phi_U={}&p\lor\bigl(\top\land\Next\Bigl(
  ((x\le5)\land\Unch(x)\land\top)\Until\\[-1mm]
&\qquad((x\le5)\land p)\Bigr)\bigr),\\[-1mm]
\Phi_R={}&\neg p\land\bigl(\bot\lor(\Reset(y)\land\\[-1mm]
&\qquad\Next(\neg p\WeakUntil((y\ge2)\lor(\bot\land\neg p))))\bigr),\\[-1mm]
\T(f)={}&\Phi_U\land\Phi_R.
\end{align*}
The upper-bounded Until uses $x$ as a preservation clock. The lower test
$y\ge2$ is the complement of the strict source bound $<2$; the Release clause
therefore permits $p$ beginning exactly at age two.

\item A Spot backend output for $\T(f)$ is shown on the left of
Fig.~\ref{fig:translation-steps45}. Its cube labels illustrate that clock
guards, reset markers, preservation markers, and action atoms are independent
propositions at this stage. In particular, cubes containing both $p$ and
$\neg p$ are propositionally satisfiable to the backend. The diagram is an
illustrative output, not a state-size or performance result.

\item Relaxation removes negative literals over extended clock atoms, and
reset completion assigns resets according to the clock class: $x$ is reset
unless the transition carries $\Unch(x)$, while $y$ is reset exactly when it
carries $\Reset(y)$. The right side of Fig.~\ref{fig:translation-steps45}
shows the resulting TBA transitions after the markers are removed. This is
the boundary of the translation theorem; subsequent optimization removes
the unsatisfiable action cubes and simplifies the automaton.
\end{enumerate}
